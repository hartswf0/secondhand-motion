Doodle to Footage

Quick, Draw! sketches and Moving Image Archive shots placed in one CLIP embedding space, run in the browser. Six steps from matching toward moving drawings.

Replay

Each best drawing is redrawn stroke by stroke, in the order the person drew it, over the archive frame it matches most closely.

Stone 1

Which drawings read best

All recognized drawings for the category, ranked by CLIP against “a drawing of a …”. Top eight, then the two lowest.

Stone 2

Drawing to footage

Nearest archive shots for each of the top three drawings, searched across all twenty categories. Raw CLIP matches line art to line-like frames; removing each modality's average vector fixes most of that.

Stone 3

Footage to its doodle twin

The archive's top three shots for the category, each paired with the closest drawing among all 5,546. A red tag means the twin is from another category.

Stone 4

A sketch as a search query

The same archive shots retrieved three ways. The sketch query is the average of the top eight drawings, with no words involved.

Stone 5

Matches across categories

The strongest pairs where a drawing's nearest shot belongs to a different category. These show what the model treats as the same shape.

Stone 6

One map for both

Gap-corrected embeddings projected to two dimensions: 160 best drawings and 918 shots. The selected category is drawn in full colour.

Where this leads

Pairs for training. Stone 3 gives drawing–shot pairs with a similarity score for every category. That is the reward signal a stroke-drawing agent would optimise, and the conditioning data a sketch-to-video model would train on.
Motion. The shots are clips, not stills. Embedding several frames per shot, or the clip with a video model, would let a drawing match a movement as well as a shape.
Scale. This run used 20 categories and a 1% sample of Quick, Draw!. The same browser pipeline extends to all 345 categories.
