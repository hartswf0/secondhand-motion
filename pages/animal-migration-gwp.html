Animal-migration, the Giraffe Walks Past way

Animal-migration from the archive, made the way Giraffe Walks Past was made. SAM 2.1 traced the animal-migration in every frame. The rig is the chosen Quick, Draw! doodle itself (quickdraw:6280704109314048): its own lines, bent at a few joints, so every frame is the same drawing. 56 keys were placed by eye over the silhouettes; in-betweens follow the silhouette frame by frame. Scenery is real Quick, Draw! doodles on depth planes that slide with the measured camera; the silhouette is painted as a cel.

Round 1 · bar_metNamed “A flock of flying birds (geese), drawn as the c.png group of four M-shaped birds repeated in three clusters.”; motion smooth. Each cluster is the c.png four-bird drawing with its own stroke habits, but repeating it three times in identical layout reads as a stamped pattern rather than one flock.
Round 2 · bar_metNamed “A flock of geese (M-shaped bird marks) taking off from the ground and flying up into the sky.”; motion broken. The birds use the doodle's open single-stroke M and arch shapes in small groups, though in dense frames overlapping marks merge into clotted shapes the doodle does not have.
Round 3 · bar_metNamed “A flock of geese taking off from a riverbank, each bird drawn as the doodle's arch or m-shaped wing stroke.”; motion broken. The birds are drawn with the doodle's arch and m strokes at a comparable weight, though the late-frame flock is so dense that individual doodle birds are hard to pick out from the tree crowns behind them.
Round 4 · bar_metNamed “A flock of geese (M-shaped bird doodles) taking off from a field and flying up into the sky.”; motion smooth. The birds use the doodle's M-shaped wing strokes at varied sizes and read as the doodle's birds multiplied into a flock; only the frame-4 switch from flat dashes to M shapes for all birds at the same moment looks like a redraw rather than wings opening.
Round 5 · bar_metNamed “a flock of geese taking off from a field, drawn as the doodle's groups of one large M bird over three small m birds”; motion smooth. The birds are the doodle's M and m marks, but they are repeated as near-identical four-bird stamps, so the late flock looks tiled rather than like the doodle's loose single grouping.
